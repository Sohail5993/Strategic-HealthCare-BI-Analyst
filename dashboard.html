Where cost concentrates

The top 20% of patients by cost account for roughly 75% of total spend — a classic long-tail distribution that makes targeted intervention far more efficient than population-wide programs.

Average cost by diagnosis category

Infectious disease and injury-related admissions carry the highest average cost per encounter — driven primarily by length of stay, not diagnosis alone.

Length of stay by comorbidity burden

Average LOS more than doubles from 3.0 to 6.8 days as comorbidity count rises from 0 to 4 (Kruskal-Wallis p < 0.001).

Cost driver model

A simple linear model (cost ~ length of stay + comorbidity count + age) explains 43% of cost variation (R² = 0.43):

The negative comorbidity coefficient isn't a data error — it reflects that comorbidity drives cost mainly through length of stay (correlation 0.66 with LOS vs. only 0.11 directly with cost), a common pattern once you control for the mediating variable.

The cohort: who gets the outreach call

Cohort definition: annual cost ≥ 90th percentile ($60,345) and at least 3 comorbidities on any encounter. 149 patients (3.7% of the population) meet both criteria — and they carry roughly double the readmission risk of everyone else, making them the clearest target for a limited-capacity care-management program.

Recommendations

  1. Target the 149-patient cohort first. They represent 25% of spend and nearly half of them are readmitted within 30 days — the highest-leverage group for a capacity-constrained outreach team.
  2. Treat length of stay as the primary cost lever, not comorbidity count directly. Since comorbidity's cost effect runs mainly through LOS, interventions that shorten stay for complex patients (discharge planning, case management) likely have more direct cost impact than comorbidity-focused programs alone.
  3. Fix data capture at the source for gender, age, and LOS fields — the standardization work here (10 gender variants, 3 date formats, impossible values) is a recurring cost of poor upstream data entry, not a one-time cleanup.
  4. Re-run the cohort definition quarterly. Patients move in and out of the high-cost/high-complexity group as conditions resolve or progress — a static list goes stale.

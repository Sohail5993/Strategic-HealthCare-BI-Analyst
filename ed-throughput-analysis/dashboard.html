Emergency Department Throughput Bottleneck Analysis

Traces where patients lose time between arrival and physician contact — door-to-triage, triage-to-room, room-to-physician — and simulates a staffing change against a limited budget to find where one added provider cuts wait time the most, rather than spreading headcount thin across every hour. Built on a synthetic 20,000-visit annual ED dataset calibrated to published national ED-operations benchmarks.

Where the time actually goes: stage breakdown by shift

Historical descriptive analysis (SQL, n=19,867 visits). Evening shift (16:00–24:00) runs 2.3x longer door-to-provider than overnight, and the gap is driven most by room-to-physician time — not triage or bed placement.

Resource utilization by shift (ρ)

Physician capacity is the binding constraint on Day and Evening shifts — utilization near the high-risk threshold where queueing delay grows sharply.

LWBS rate by shift

Left-without-being-seen concentrates almost entirely in the Evening shift — 24x the overnight rate.

Simulation: baseline vs. recommended intervention

Recommendations

  1. Add one physician-equivalent (APP/PA) to the Evening shift (16:00–24:00), not more triage nurses. Utilization analysis shows triage and bed capacity have headroom; physician bandwidth is the binding constraint driving both wait time and LWBS.
  2. Pilot a low-acuity fast-track lane staffed by the added APP to pull ESI 4–5 patients out of the main physician queue, protecting capacity for higher-acuity patients.
  3. Re-run this utilization analysis quarterly as volume shifts seasonally — the Evening bottleneck is a snapshot of current demand, not a fixed structural fact.
  4. Track LWBS rate as a leading indicator alongside door-to-provider time; it responded more strongly (-60% relative) than average wait time (-21%) in the simulation, making it a more sensitive signal of crowding relief.
Synthetic data, calibrated to published national ED-operations benchmarks (ACEP / CDC NHAMCS ranges). Not derived from any real hospital's records.
